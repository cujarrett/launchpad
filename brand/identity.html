You declare what you want. The platform puts it in the air.

The idea

Brackets, not a rocket.

A rocket is the thing you asked to get away from - it's what the stock emoji already says, and it describes cargo rather than the product. Launchpad isn't the vehicle; it's the declaration that puts something in the air. So the mark is a pair of brackets - the punctuation of every manifest the platform commits - holding an arrow on its way up.

It reads three ways at once: declaration (the brackets), deployment (the arrow), and containment - the platform's actual promise, that the thing going up is held inside guardrails you didn't have to build.

It survives the tab bar

A favicon is where most marks die. The weight contrast does the work: hairline brackets, heavy arrow - so even at 18 px the silhouette stays two distinct shapes instead of one grey smudge.

The wordmark is set in the same monospace the app already uses for every resource name - the product's own voice, rather than a display face borrowed from somewhere else. The tonal shift on pad marks the half that matters.

What it beat

Four rounds. The whole "shape resting on a bar" family - the obvious way to draw a launch pad - had to be abandoned outright: every version collapsed into the eject symbol once it hit favicon size.

The alternate

trajectory

If you want warmth over precision.

A payload leaving a pad on an arc. Softer and more optimistic than the brackets, and it holds up small - but it trades away the declarative idea, and an arc reads as generic motion in a way brackets never do. Worth keeping on the shelf; not what I'd ship.
